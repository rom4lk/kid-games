# Minecraft: a description of the original game

## The game in brief

Minecraft is an open-world sandbox made almost entirely of cubic blocks. The player can break blocks, get resources out of them and place new blocks, gradually changing the surrounding world. The game does not set a single correct path: you can explore, build, mine materials, grow plants, create machines or invent your own goals.

The specific items and the balance differ between editions and versions of Minecraft, but the basic mechanics are shared.

## The main game loop

1. Explore the world and find a useful material.
2. Mine the resource with a suitable tool.
3. Turn the resources into items or building blocks.
4. Use what you got for building, exploring or mining rarer materials.
5. Expand the base and set yourself the next goal.

This loop has no required ending. The player decides when a task is done and what to do next.

## The main mechanics

### A world of blocks

- The world is divided into blocks with constant properties.
- Most blocks can be broken, picked up and placed somewhere else.
- Building follows a simple spatial grid, yet allows a huge number of combinations.
- The result of an action stays in the world: a dug mine, a built house or a bridge becomes part of the map.

### Exploration

- The world is generated procedurally and includes different biomes, caves, bodies of water and ready-made structures.
- New places give new resources, creatures and tasks.
- Navigating and getting back to the base safely become a separate part of the game.

### Mining and tools

- Different materials are mined at different speeds and sometimes require a specific tool.
- Tools form a clear progression ladder: better materials unlock faster mining and new resources.
- Durability makes you craft replacement tools from time to time.

### Crafting

- Resources are combined by recipes on a crafting grid.
- The player creates tools, weapons, armor, building blocks and useful devices.
- A furnace turns one material into another at the cost of fuel and time.

### Building

- Blocks can be placed freely in the world wherever there is room.
- Builds can be practical, decorative or both.
- The game rarely evaluates a build formally: its quality and purpose are decided by the player.

### Survival

- Survival mode has health, hunger, falling, fire, drowning and other dangers.
- Day turns into night, and hostile creatures appear more often at night.
- After death the player respawns but may lose the contents of their inventory.
- Food, shelter, lighting and gear reduce the risk.

### Combat and creatures

- The player meets peaceful, neutral and hostile creatures.
- Melee and ranged combat, armor, shields, potions and enchantments are available.
- Animals can be bred, some creatures can be tamed, and villagers can be traded with.

### Farming and production

- Plants can be planted, grown and harvested.
- Animals and farms give renewable resources.
- Many processes can be automated and combined into production chains.

### Redstone

- Redstone carries a signal between blocks and makes logic circuits possible.
- Simple components add up to doors, traps, transport lines, clocks and automatic farms.
- The system supports experimenting: the player sees the circuit working right in the world and fixes it step by step.

### Progression and long-term goals

- Rare materials, enchantments and new dimensions give a long-term direction.
- The game has special opponents and nominal final achievements, but the world stays available afterwards.
- The achievement system offers landmarks without replacing the freedom of choice.

### Creative mode and multiplayer

- In creative mode resources are unlimited, dangers do not interfere with building, and the player can fly.
- In multiplayer, participants build, explore, trade resources or compete.
- Servers and custom maps can change the goals and the rules of the game completely.

## Why this combination of mechanics works

The main quality of Minecraft is the link between simple actions and great freedom. A mined block is at once a resource, a building part and a way to change space. A small goal like making a pickaxe naturally leads to exploring a mine, building a base and looking for new materials. The game offers clear local actions but hardly limits the player's overall plan.
